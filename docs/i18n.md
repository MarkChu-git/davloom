# Languages

The Dashboard speaks English and Simplified Chinese (`zh-CN`). This page says how it chooses
between them, where its words live, and which Chinese term stands for each of Davloom's own.

## Choosing the language

- With nothing chosen, the Dashboard follows the browser: any Chinese in `navigator.languages`, in
  any script or region (`zh`, `zh-CN`, `zh-TW`, `zh-Hant-HK`), shows Simplified Chinese, and
  anything else shows English.
- Settings > Appearance > Language offers English, 简体中文 and System. The choice is kept in this
  browser's `localStorage` under `davloom-language`, applies at once to every open tab, and System
  removes it. Without storage, as in a private window, a choice lasts until the page closes.
- `lang` on `<html>` always names the language shown, so screen readers and the browser's own
  features, such as hyphenation and font choice, read it right.

## First paint

Each prerendered page holds its frame twice, once in each language, in elements marked
`data-frame-locale`. A small script in `<head>` (`apps/web/src/features/i18n/script.ts`) sets `lang`
on `<html>` from the stored choice or the browser before `<body>` is parsed, and for Chinese it also
writes the page's Chinese title and description. The stylesheet shows only the frame whose language
matches `lang`, so a Chinese reader never sees English first. Once the code loads, the page renders
in one language only.

## Where the words live

- `apps/web/src/features/i18n/messages/en.ts` holds every English word of the Dashboard, by key.
  Braces hold a placeholder, such as `{name}`, and a message whose words depend on a number has
  `one` and `other` forms, which `Intl.PluralRules` chooses between. Chinese has only `other`.
- `apps/web/src/features/i18n/messages/zh-CN.ts` holds the same keys in Chinese. TypeScript fails
  when a key is missing, and `i18n.test.tsx` fails when a text is empty or its placeholders or tags
  differ from the English.
- `apps/web/src/features/i18n/messages/astryx-zh-CN.ts` supplies the words of Astryx components
  that Astryx's own Chinese catalog leaves out.
- A component reads its words with `const t = useT()` and `t("key", { name })`. Numbers, sizes,
  dates, times, relative times and lists go through `t.count`, `t.bytes`, `t.date`, `t.dateTime`,
  `t.relative` and `t.list`, which format them with `Intl` for the language shown.
- A message that holds a link marks it with a tag, such as `<link>Credentials</link>`, so a
  translation can move the link within its sentence; `RichText` renders it.

To add words, add the key to `en.ts` and to `zh-CN.ts`. Write a whole sentence per key rather
than joining fragments, because Chinese orders a sentence's parts differently.

## Style

Write Chinese that is short and plain, as a native product would say it. Use full-width punctuation
(`，。：；？！“”（）`) and leave a space between Chinese and a Latin word or a number, as in
`3 个文件` or `Davloom 实例`. Address the reader as 你, and keep error messages to what happened and
what to do next.
